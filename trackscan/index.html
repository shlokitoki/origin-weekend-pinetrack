How Pinetrack works

From a shake on the train to an alert for the track team, with no one watching a screen.

  1. 01Sensor on the train floorRecords vibration 100 times a second plus GPS on every trip. Today, phones stand in for our sensor.
  2. 02Uploads after each runUploads over cellular after each run, automatically. Today, the phone exports were uploaded by hand.
  3. 03Cleans the signalRemoves gravity, drops braking and accelerating, and adjusts for speed.
  4. 04Pins it to the track mapPins each second to the official track map and compares it with earlier passes in the same direction.
  5. 05Checks the ruleFlags a 25 m slice when it is at least 1.5× typical and elevated (top 10% for that pass) on at least 2 passes, at least 2/3 of the passes covering it. This is the exact rule behind the spots on the map.
  6. 06Alerts track maintenanceSends an automatic alert to the agency’s track maintenance team (email, SMS or work order) with location, evidence and suggested checks. An inspector confirms or dismisses it, and that feedback is saved. In this demo, alerts go to our team inbox.

Alert levels

  • WatchElevated on 1 pass. Logged, no alert.
  • InspectMeets the rule in step 5. Alert sent.
  • UrgentComing with continuous monitoring Fires when a spot gets sharply worse week over week, so it needs trend history.

        How we measured it

        Pinetrack finds places worth inspecting. It does not diagnose broken rails. Phone vibration can reflect the vehicle, speed, track geometry, crossings, or other conditions. An inspector must establish the cause.

        Westbound and eastbound recordings are analyzed separately. Multiple phones on the same inferred pass contribute one combined result. A colored ribbon is an approximate corridor location, not a surveyed rail position or confirmed track assignment.
